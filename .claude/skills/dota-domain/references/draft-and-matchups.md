# Draft, Counters, Synergies, Matchups

## Draft phases

Competitive/ranked drafting alternates bans and picks in a fixed order
(exact format depends on game mode — All Pick has no bans/order in
unranked, Captain's Mode/ranked has structured ban/pick phases). Two
generally useful framings regardless of exact mode:

- **Early picks** commit to a role/archetype before full information about
  the enemy draft is available — they should be flexible or individually
  strong enough to justify that risk.
- **Late picks** have more information (can react to enemy picks directly)
  but less draft flexibility (remaining hero pool is smaller).

When reasoning about "was this a good pick," account for what was known
at the time it was made — a pick that looks bad against the final enemy
draft may have been the best available choice given only what was visible
then.

## Counters

"Hero A counters hero B" is a directional, situational claim, not a fixed
property of two heroes. It usually reduces to one or more of:
- A's kit directly punishes B's core mechanic (e.g. spell immunity denies
  a disable-reliant hero's core value; a silence denies a spellcaster)
- A out-scales or out-tempos B in the way the current draft is likely to
  play out
- A has itemization options that specifically answer B's damage type/kit,
  which B's team doesn't have an easy answer to

A "counter pick" recommendation should identify *which* of these
mechanisms applies, not just cite a counter-pick statistic. Two heroes
being generically labeled counters doesn't guarantee it holds in a
specific draft/itemization/skill context.

## Synergies

Synergy = one hero's presence measurably improves another's effectiveness
beyond their individual strength — e.g. an initiator whose disable is
immediately followed by a nuker's burst combo, or an aura/buff hero
paired with a hero that benefits disproportionately from that buff.

Like counters, synergy claims should be traceable to a specific mechanism
(this ability enables that ability/build), not just "these heroes are
picked together often."

## Matchup reasoning

When asked to reason about a matchup (lane matchup, hero vs hero, or draft
vs draft), prefer decomposing into concrete mechanisms over a holistic
"good/bad" verdict:
- damage type and mitigation options on both sides
- range/mobility differences that affect who controls engagements
- level/gold thresholds where the matchup flips (e.g. "even until level 6,
  favors B after that" due to an ultimate)
- itemization counterplay available to each side, and whether it's
  actually affordable in the relevant timeframe

## Facets and talents

Facets and talents are hero-specific *modifiers* layered on top of a
hero's base kit — they can meaningfully shift a hero's role, damage type,
or archetype, sometimes enough to change which of the reasoning above
applies. Treat facet/talent choice as part of the hero's effective kit for
a given game, not a minor detail:
- a talent that changes an ability's damage type changes what mitigates it
- a facet that adds/removes a mechanic (e.g. adds a disable, changes a
  scaling stat) can change what counters/synergies apply
- talent/facet choice usually depends on the matchup and game plan, so
  "which talent is generally best" is a weaker question than "which talent
  serves this hero's role in this game"

Because facets/talents change over patches and per-hero, don't assume
specifics from memory when precision matters — verify against current
patch data rather than asserting exact values.
